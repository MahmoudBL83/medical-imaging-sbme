In the same way that computed tomography is the 3-D tomographic modality
based on projection radiography, SPECT is the 3-D nuclear medicine modality
based on planar scintigraphy. In Figure III.2, the ﬁrst four rows are short axis
slices (as though the heart were sliced like a bread loaf), the next two rows
are horizontal long axisslices (orthogonal to the short axis), and the ﬁnal two
rows are vertical long axis slices (orthogonal to the short axis and also to the
horizontal long axis). These slices thus represent orientations relative to the
heart, not the long axis of the (entire) body.
Arguably the most advanced form of nuclear medicine imaging is positron
emission tomography (PET), which utilizes specialized radiotracers and instru-
mentation. Figure III.3 depicts a whole-body PET scan (in anterior, posterior,
and left lateral reprojection views) of the distribution of ﬂuorine-18-labeled
deoxyglucose (FDG), whose distribution reﬂects glucose metabolism. FDG-PET
is the most commonly performed PET study with a focus on cancer detection
and staging. These reprojection views are common in FDG-PET and are formed
from the 3-D reconstructed dataset.
