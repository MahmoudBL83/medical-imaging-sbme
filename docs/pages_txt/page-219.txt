Sinogram An image of g(ℓ, θ) with ℓ and θ as rectilinear coordinates is called
a sinogram. It is a pictorial representation of the Radon transform off (x, y) and
represents the data that are necessary to reconstruct f (x, y).
An object and its sinogram are shown in Figure 6.9. It is important to
observe several features about this sinogram. First, by convention in this book,
the bottommost row of the sinogram corresponds to the projection of the
object at θ = 0. With reference to Figure 6.7, we see that this is the projection
comprising vertical integrals of the object. Since the object in Figure 6.9(a) is
widest in the horizontal direction, this gives the widest projection, which is
evident in Figure 6.9(b).

are increasingly narrower and become narrowest at the angle θ = π/2. This
