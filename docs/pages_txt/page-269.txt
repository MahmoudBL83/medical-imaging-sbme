A ﬁnal and somewhat subtle desirable characteristic for gamma emitters is
that they should have monoenergetic (single energy) decay. The reason for this
is that with monoenergetic emissions, energy-sensitive detection can be used to
discriminate the primary photons from those that have been Compton scattered.
It can be seen from Table 7.1 that most of these common radionuclides are
monoenergetic. Even those that are not monoenergetic, such as Tl-201 and
Ga-67, emit only two or three different energy photons, in contrast to a typical
polyenergetic x-ray beam, which has a continuous spectrum of energies.
